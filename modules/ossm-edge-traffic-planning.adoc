= Plan your ingress and egress strategy

[role="_abstract"]

A service mesh secures and manages traffic between services, but most applications also need to accept traffic from external clients or send traffic to external services.
Gateways are the mechanism that gives you control over this traffic at the mesh boundary.

Without gateways, external traffic can still reach mesh services through direct pod access or {ocp-short-name} routes, but none of the mesh's routing rules, security policies, or observability features apply to that traffic.
Gateways provide a managed entry and exit point where you can enforce policies and monitor traffic as it crosses the boundary:

* An ingress gateway accepts traffic from external clients and routes it to services inside the mesh.
* An egress gateway routes traffic from mesh services to external destinations.

== Security at the mesh boundary

Mutual TLS (mTLS) encrypts traffic automatically between proxies inside the mesh, but it is a proxy-to-proxy protocol.
mTLS does not extend to external clients or services that are not part of the mesh.

At the gateway boundary, you secure external connections by configuring standard TLS certificates on the gateway itself.
Gateways can also require authentication of external requests and enforce authorization policies to control which external traffic is allowed into the mesh.
