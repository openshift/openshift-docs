= DNS failover with groups

[role="_abstract"]
You can use DNS groups to switch DNS traffic between clusters during a planned migration or an outage. By preparing a standby deployment and assigning its DNS operator to a separate group, you can activate that group when you need to move traffic to the standby deployment.

Group::
A group is a method of tagging a dns-operator to reconcile on resources. Each DNS operator can belong to one group at a time. The DNS Operator can be enabled and disabled by modifying the TXT records with the DNS provider.

The DNS operator's group assignment and the active-groups TXT record determine how the operator manages DNS records:

Active group::
A group listed in the active-groups TXT record. DNS operators configured with an active group manage DNS records on their local clusters, as well as any delegated records in a multicluster setup. They also remove DNS records associated with inactive groups. 

Inactive group::
A group that is not listed in the active-groups TXT record. DNS operators in an inactive group do not attempt to clean up their DNS records because their clusters might be unavailable during an outage. Instead, DNS operators in active groups clean up these records in the DNS provider.

Ungrouped operator::
A DNS operator with no group assignment. An ungrouped operator reconciles the `DNSRecord` custom resource (CRs) it can access, regardless of the active-groups TXT record. It does not perform cleanup for inactive groups. 

[id="dns-group-failover-behavior_{context}"]
== How group failover works

You initiate group failover by updating the active-groups TXT record, either through the DNS CLI or directly in the DNS provider.

For example, a primary cluster and a standby cluster can have `DNSRecord` CRs for the same host, with each cluster's records pointing to its own service addresses. The primary cluster's DNS operator is in an active group, and the standby cluster's DNS operator is in an inactive group. 
If the primary cluster becomes unavailable, you can activate the standby group by updating the TXT record. Then, remove the primary group from the active list. Operators in the standby group reconcile their records and clean up provider records associated with the inactive primary group. 
Keep at least one group with an available cluster active so that reconciliation and cleanup can continue.

The DNS operator checks the active-groups TXT record during scheduled reconciliation. A change to the active groups takes effect during a reconciliation cycle, so the failover is no immediate.
